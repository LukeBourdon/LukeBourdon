# Core learning materials and facilitator notes

## Session 1 - pause, verify, report (20 minutes)

**0–3 minutes:** Explain how a plausible work request can create harm. An urgent tone or familiar logo is not verification. Staff should feel able to report mistakes quickly.

**3–8 minutes:** Show the fictional document-share message in [campaign design](campaign-design.md). Ask learners to identify the unexpected request, unfamiliar destination and pressure to act. On mobile, do not require risky clicking to inspect a link: use the reporting route or independently open the known service.

**8–13 minutes:** Practise three actions: open the known service directly; verify through an independently known person/contact; report using the organisation's published route. If you clicked, stop interacting and contact the service desk immediately. If you entered a password, say so promptly so IT can revoke sessions and reset safely. Never approve an MFA prompt you did not initiate.

**13–17 minutes:** Demonstrate a named, expiring file share and explain why forwarding customer data to a personal account is prohibited. Use synthetic data only.

**17–20 minutes:** Complete the five-question check below, discuss reasoning and give accessible written takeaways.

## Knowledge check - facilitator answer key

1. An unexpected document says you must sign in today. What is the best first action? A: sign in quickly; B: open the known service independently or verify via a known route; C: forward to a personal account. **B** - validate the request without following the supplied path.
2. You clicked a suspicious link but entered nothing. A: delete the email; B: report immediately and describe the action; C: wait for a problem. **B** - the response team needs timely facts.
3. Your phone shows an MFA approval you did not request. A: approve to stop prompts; B: deny and report; C: ask a colleague to approve. **B** - unexpected prompts can indicate attempted misuse.
4. A supplier emails new bank details. A: use the email's phone number; B: independently call the known supplier contact and obtain dual approval; C: accept the signature as proof. **B** - verify outside the potentially compromised channel.
5. A customer export must be shared externally. A: public link; B: personal storage; C: owner-approved named access with appropriate protection and expiry. **C** - share only the necessary data with authorised recipients.

Pass: four of five correct. The facilitator discusses each wrong answer and repeats with a new scenario. Record completion and score, not passwords or personal examples.

## Role exercises

| Audience | Scenario | Expected behaviour | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finance | Urgent bank-detail change before payment run | Known-number callback and second approver; hold if unverified | Completed mock verification record |
| HR | Manager asks for employee health export | Verify purpose, minimum data, Restricted handling and authorised recipients | Correct fictional sharing decision |
| Engineering | Secret discovered in repository history | Report, revoke/rotate, investigate use and remove exposure through controlled process | Tabletop action sequence; deleting a file alone is insufficient |
| Support | Caller requests another customer's ticket history | Verify identity and entitlement; refuse unverified export | Role-play decision and escalation |
| Leadership | Suspected breach before an important client meeting | Escalate and preserve evidence; do not delay notification assessment for reputation reasons | Tabletop decision log |

## Takeaway text

Pause when a request is unexpected. Verify through a route you already trust. Report quickly, including when you clicked. Keep passwords and MFA approvals private. Check recipients and permissions before sharing. Ask for help when uncertain.
